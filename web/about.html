🐾 About Animal Tracker Map

A map of where 24 of the world's best-loved animals have been seen, and the journeys some of them make. It's free, has no ads, and is built entirely from open data.

Where the data comes from

Sightings
Records shared through GBIF, the Global Biodiversity Information Facility, by museums, research projects and people using apps like iNaturalist. Refreshed every Monday.
Migration tracks
GPS tags fitted by researchers and published on Movebank, used under each study's licence.
Animal profiles and photos
Text from Wikipedia under CC BY-SA 4.0, and photos from Wikimedia Commons under their own licences, listed below.
The map
Map tiles from OpenFreeMap, built with OpenMapTiles from OpenStreetMap data, and drawn with MapLibre.

What the map can and can't tell you

  • Dots show where an animal has been recorded, not how many live there. Up to about 3,000 records are sampled per animal and spread across its countries, so busy areas look thinner than they are and remote areas with few observers look empty.
  • Locations are rounded to about 10 km. That's enough for a world map, and it avoids pointing to the exact places threatened animals live.
  • Zoo animals are left out, along with museum specimens and records from countries far outside an animal's range. Some mistakes may remain; for example, herded reindeer in Scandinavia can't be told apart from wild caribou.
  • Not every animal has tracks. Tracks appear only where researchers have published tagging data that can be reused, and each journey is simplified to one location per day.
  • Older records are rarer. Most sightings are recent, because far more people record wildlife now, so "Before 1990" shows much less than the animal's past range. Records with no year only appear under "All years".

Your privacy

"Near me" asks your browser for your location and works out the nearest animals on your device. Your location is never sent anywhere.

The site may count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies and doesn't follow you to other sites.
